---
title: "Why this site has a workshop, not a discography"
date: "2026-05-01"
excerpt: "Finished songs are the least interesting part of making songs."
tags: ["music", "process"]
readTime: "4 min"
---

Every musician's website has the same shape: a hero photo, a Spotify embed, a tour list. A discography is a museum — everything in it is finished, lit, and behind glass.

This site keeps a workshop instead. The album page shows songs *while they're being made*: the demo with placeholder lyrics, the note admitting the verses don't work yet, the story of where the chorus arrived. When a song is done it graduates to the player; until then you can watch it grow up.

Two reasons this is worth the embarrassment:

1. **Process is the product now.** Anyone can publish a finished track. The thing a listener can't get anywhere else is watching a specific person make specific decisions.
2. **It keeps me honest.** A song that's been "almost done" on a public page for three months is a song that's asking to be either finished or killed.

The template this site is built from ships with the workshop as a first-class feature — edit `content/album.json` and the page updates. Museum optional.
